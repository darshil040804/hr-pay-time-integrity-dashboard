# User Guide

This guide is for HR and payroll staff who use the dashboard to find and fix data problems before payday. You don't need any technical knowledge to use it.

> **About the data:** everything in this dashboard is made up. The employees, jobs, and pay are fictional, generated by a program to look like a university's HR system. Names are randomly generated, and any resemblance to real people is coincidental.

## What the dashboard does

Before each payday, HR records, time sheets, and payroll should all agree. When they don't, someone may be paid the wrong amount, or paid when they shouldn't be. The dashboard runs 11 automatic checks and lists every record that fails one. We call each of these an **exception**.

For each exception you'll see:
- **who** it affects (employee ID and name),
- **what** is wrong, in a plain sentence with the actual numbers,
- **what to do**, in the *Suggested action* column.

The checks run by themselves every time you open a page. There is nothing to start or refresh.

## Getting around

Use the menu on the left:

| Page | Use it to |
|---|---|
| **app** (Home) | See the total number of exceptions and how many are High, Medium, and Low. |
| **Org Personnel** | Fix problems with positions, org units, and employee classifications. |
| **Time Payroll** | Compare time sheets with payroll for each pay period, and fix pay errors. |
| **HR Overview** | See headcount, vacancies, and how exceptions are spread across pay periods. |
| **Testing and QA** | See how the checks themselves are tested. Mostly of interest to auditors and analysts. |
| **About** | Read how the checks work and find the other documents. |

## What to fix first

Every exception has a **severity**:

| Severity | What it means | When to act |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Someone may be paid wrongly, or a wage-and-hour (FLSA) rule is broken. | Before the next pay run. |
| **Medium** | A record is wrong and could lead to pay or reporting errors. | This pay period. |
| **Low** | A data-quality issue with no direct effect on pay. | When you have time. |

Tables are already sorted with High first.

## Org & Personnel page

This page checks the org structure and who is assigned where.

1. The **bar chart** shows how many exceptions each check found.
2. Under **Exception details**, narrow the list with the three filters:
   - **Rule**: one or more checks.
   - **Severity**: for example, only High.
   - **Org unit**: only your department(s).

   Leaving a filter empty means "show all".
3. Read the **Issue** and **Suggested action** for each row, and make the correction in the HR system.
4. Click **Download Excel** to save the rows you're looking at as a spreadsheet. This is useful as a work list or to send to a department.

The checks on this page:

| Check | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **OP-01** Employee in an inactive position | Someone is still assigned to a position that has been closed. |
| **OP-02** Position with no valid org unit | A position isn't attached to a real department, so it won't appear in department reports. |
| **OP-03** Two active employees in one position | A position is meant for one person. The second person is probably assigned to the wrong position. |
| **OP-04** Terminated employee still in a position | Someone has left but their position wasn't released, so it can't be refilled and looks occupied. |
| **OP-05** Hourly/Salaried doesn't match the job | The job is Exempt (salaried, no overtime) or Non-exempt (hourly, overtime eligible), but the employee is set up the other way. This affects overtime eligibility. |

## Time & Payroll page

This page compares the hours people recorded with the hours payroll paid.

1. Choose a **Pay period** at the top, or leave it on *All pay periods*.
2. The four numbers show hours recorded, hours paid, the difference, and the number of exceptions for that period.
3. The **left chart** compares recorded and paid hours for each period. The **right chart** shows the difference. A bar above zero means more hours were paid than recorded.
4. Filter the **Exception details** table by **Rule** and **Severity**, then **Download Excel** if needed.

> **Note:** "Hours paid" counts regular, overtime, vacation, and sick pay. Shift differential isn't counted, because it's an extra amount paid on hours that are already counted as regular.

The checks on this page:

| Check | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **TP-01** Paid hours don't match recorded hours | Payroll paid more or fewer hours than the time sheet shows (differences of half an hour or less are ignored). |
| **TP-02** Overtime paid to an Exempt employee | Exempt employees aren't eligible for overtime, so this is likely an overpayment. |
| **TP-03** Hourly employee paid with no time sheet | Pay was issued with no hours recorded to support it. |
| **TP-04** More than 16 or negative hours in a day | Almost always a typing mistake on the time sheet. |
| **TP-05** Duplicate pay line | The same type of pay appears twice for one person in one period. They may have been paid twice. |
| **TP-06** Paid after termination | A payment is dated after the person left. Payment on the termination date itself is fine. |

## HR Overview page

| Number | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Headcount** | Employees currently active. |
| **Active positions** | Positions currently open for use. |
| **Vacancy rate** | Share of active positions with nobody in them. |
| **Total exceptions** | All exceptions from all 11 checks. |
| **Employees with any exception** | Share of all employees (current and former) who appear in at least one exception. |

Below these are headcount by department, and a chart of Time & Payroll exceptions per pay period. A period with a tall bar deserves a closer look. Org & Personnel checks aren't tied to a pay period, so they don't appear in that chart.

## A typical review before payday

1. Open **Home** and note the number of **High** exceptions.
2. On **Time & Payroll**, select the current pay period and set **Severity** to High. Work through each row using its suggested action.
3. On **Org & Personnel**, filter **Severity** to High (OP-04 and OP-05) and fix those records.
4. Download the remaining Medium and Low exceptions to Excel and share them with the departments concerned.

## Tips

- **Charts:** hover over any bar to see its exact value. Click **View chart data** under a chart to see the numbers as a table.
- **Light or dark:** to switch, open the **⋮** menu at the top right and choose **Settings**.
- **Where the data comes from:** the data is fixed. Fixing a record means correcting it in the HR or payroll system, so the exception won't disappear from this demo dashboard.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Exception** | A record that fails one of the checks. |
| **Org unit** | A department or division in the organization chart. |
| **Position** | A specific seat in an org unit (for example, "Payroll Specialist in the Payroll Office"). One person per position. |
| **Job** | A general role that positions are based on, with an FLSA classification. |
| **Exempt / Non-exempt** | US wage-and-hour (FLSA) classification. Non-exempt employees are paid hourly and are owed overtime; Exempt employees are salaried and are not. |
| **Employee subgroup** | How the employee is set up for pay: Salaried or Hourly. It should match the job's FLSA classification. |
| **Pay period** | A two-week window (PP01 to PP06) whose time is paid on the following Friday. |
| **Wage type** | A kind of pay: 1000 Regular, 1100 Overtime, 1200 Vacation, 1300 Sick, 1400 Shift Differential. |
| **Term date** | The employee's last day. |
